D5. QR6: does it work on real lighting? MVTec AD 2

This is the experiment that matters most for credibility, because nothing in it is synthetic. AD 2's public test set shows each scene under several real lighting conditions, encoded in the file name (regular, overexposed, underexposed, shift_1…4), while train and validation images are all regular. So a bank fitted on the train set is deployed under lighting it has never seen: exactly our problem.

Protocol (scripts/qr6_natural.py). Per category: bank from train/good (vial 291 images, sheet_metal 137, can 412); source threshold from validation/good. For each lighting condition L: the "operator's photos" are k random good images of condition L from test_public; evaluation uses the remaining good images of L plus all defective images of L (calibration photos are never scored). All (or 40 random) k-subsets are averaged because only 4 (sheet_metal), 5 (vial) or 12 (can) good photos exist per condition. Normals from the validation split only would have been the policy for the private submission; here the photos must come from the same lighting, so the test-public good images are the only source: we state that this is an evaluation of the method, not part of the submission.

What the real data say

  • Real lighting breaks the fixed threshold, as in the synthetic study. Vial under-exposed: 100% false alarms; over-exposed: 80%; sheet_metal shift_2: 50%. The "regular" rows (the lighting the bank saw) are at 0% for vial and sheet_metal.
  • Real lighting also hurts ranking, more than our synthetic shifts did. Vial AUROC falls from 0.95 (regular) to 0.72 (over-exposed) and 0.81 (under-exposed). Over/under-exposure clips highlights and shadows: information is destroyed, not merely offset.
  • B3 works on real lighting, and the numbers follow theory even where the guarantee is void. With k photos the recalibrated false-alarm rate is 33% (k=2), 25% (k=3), 20% (k=4) on vial and 8.3–8.9% (k = 8, 11) on can, versus the theory 1/(k+1) = 33%, 25%, 20%, 11%, 8.3%. For n < 9 the guarantee is formally lost (C1) but the error is exactly the predicted 1/(n+1): useful to know before deciding how many photos to take. Two caveats: this FPR is computed on the 1–3 remaining good photos per condition (very noisy), and the price is detection: vial TPR is about 0.5 under over/under-exposure at the recalibrated threshold (0.5–0.7 over all non-regular conditions).
  • B2 does not help here. On vial B2a is neutral to slightly harmful (mean AUROC 0.880 → 0.871 at k=4; over-exposed 0.72 → 0.69), B2b is a little worse again; on sheet_metal B2a changes AUROC by +0.01…+0.02 but B2b collapses it (0.81 → 0.56–0.63) at k = 2–3 because a standard deviation cannot be estimated from 2–3 photos (consistent with QR4: B2b needs k ≥ 25). On can nothing moves, because the baseline is at chance (AUROC 0.40–0.50).
Verdict on QR6: partially supported. The decision repair (B3) transfers to real lighting on the two categories where the baseline works (vial, sheet_metal) and behaves as theory predicts. The perception repair (B2) is not demonstrated on real lighting: sign of the AUROC change is mixed (negative on vial, small positive on sheet_metal, none on can). We cannot call this a refutation, because the test has k ≤ 4 photos (the regime where QR4 already showed B2b fails and B2a has little to estimate) and only 15 defective images per condition.
Hypotheses for why B2 does not transfer (not tested). (1) Real over/under-exposure is non-linear and clips, so it is not a per-channel affine map of the features. (2) Real changes are spatially structured (highlights move across a metallic or glass surface), which a global per-channel offset cannot undo. (3) With only 4–5 photos per condition, the estimate is noisy. A fair test needs a dataset with dozens of normal photos per lighting condition; AD 2's public split does not provide that. The augmentation baseline (B1, next section) suggests that putting real or realistic lighting in the bank is the stronger lever when the shift is complex.

The AD 2 "can" scare, explained

Early in the project vanilla PatchCore on AD 2 can gave image AUROC ≈ 0.45 (below chance) and we feared a bug. We checked: the ImageNet weights load correctly (weights allclose to a fresh model); a minimal reimplementation outside the library gives the same result; per-lighting-condition AUROC is also ≈ 0.4; alternative score statistics (p99, top-10 mean, mean) do not help; and the median defect covers only 5.6×10−5 of a 2232×1024 image, which at 256 px is far smaller than one 16×16 feature cell. This agrees with the AD 2 paper (PatchCore AU-PRO0.05 ≈ 4.7% on can, and a large gain from higher input resolution). It is a resolution/defect-size effect, not a bug, and it is why our B2/B3 numbers on can are uninformative (nothing to repair). It would be the natural motivation for a crop-and-zoom front end (B5), which we did not build.
