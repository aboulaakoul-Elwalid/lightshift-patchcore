Appendix. Earlier lessons we kept (the clamp, and why raw scores matter)

Before this project, an earlier PatchCore study (on MVTec AD cable and MVTec LOCO splicing_connectors, July 2026) produced a write-up that shaped three decisions here. Source: smartex-lab-p0/docs/mmselec/MMSELEC_PATCHCORE_RAW_SCORE_WRITEUP_2026-11-12.html. Summary of what carried over:

1. Library scores can be clipped, and clipping destroys ranking

anomalib normalises raw distances to [0, 1] with clamp((raw − thr)/(max − min) + 0.5, 0, 1). Everything above the clamp collapses to 1.0 and everything below to 0.0, and ties count as ½ in AUROC. In the earlier study 57% of clean scores were pinned at exactly 0, and the "AUROC = 0.5000" rows seen under rotation were saturation, not a model verdict: 8 of 36 shift rows had exactly 0.5000, the fingerprint of saturation. Re-measured on raw distances the clean baseline was 0.833, not 0.789.

AUROC = P(sdefect > sgood) + ½ P(sdefect = sgood) → if every score clamps to 1.0, AUROC = ½ regardless of the model.

Consequence in this project: all our scores are raw squared distances (no clamp), and B2/B3 operate on them.

2. Ranking and calibration are different failures

In that study, severe warm colour shift made 63% of good parts fail at the source threshold (calibration failure) and also cost about 0.10 AUROC (ranking failure). A threshold fix helps the first and cannot help the second. This is the two-axis framing (B3 and B2) used throughout.

3. The conformal formula in the sujet was backwards

First spotted in that write-up: "k-th largest" must be the k-th smallest, otherwise n = 50, α = 0.1 flags about 90% of good parts. See C1.

Other things that write-up recorded (not re-verified here)

  • Logical defects (wrong count/position) are hard for PatchCore: AUROC 0.75 vs 0.84 for structural defects on splicing_connectors.
  • Rotation breaks PatchCore (raw AUROC 0.58–0.59), unlike lighting; this is the pose-variability issue behind the "crop the object first" idea (B5).
