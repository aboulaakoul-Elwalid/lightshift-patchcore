LightShift-PatchCore · learning report · written for study with pen and paper

Can a memory-bank detector keep its promise when the lights change?

One document, from zero. It explains the task, the maths of PatchCore, how every metric is computed, why lighting breaks the detector, the two repairs we built (a conformal threshold and a feature-statistics alignment), every experiment we ran with its numbers, what we did not do, and the MVTec AD 2 submission. Worked examples and exercises with solutions are inside. Every number comes from a file in the repository; where a claim is weaker than it sounds, we say so.

The whole project in four lines. 1) PatchCore stores what normal patches look like and scores a new image by how far its worst patch is from anything stored. 2) A lighting change moves every patch, so normal parts start to look abnormal: the ranking of images mostly survives but the fixed threshold does not. 3) B3 fixes the threshold from k normal photos of the new lighting, with a provable false-alarm rate; B2 repairs the features before matching. 4) Measured result: the false-alarm rate drops from about {{V:fpr_src_all}} to about {{V:fpr_b3_k10}} with k = 10 photos, and B2 adds detection power on top.
