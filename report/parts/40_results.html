Part D · Every experiment, with its numbers

D0. How to read the evidence

Common protocol. Per category, the good training images are split with a fixed seed: 60% bank, 10% source calibration (sets the original threshold), 30% target pool (the "operator's photos": k are drawn from here). The test set is the official MVTec AD test set (good + defective). Lighting shifts are applied to everything that is seen under the new lighting (target pool and test images). Scores are raw squared distances. α = 0.1. Results are means over 3 categories × 5 families × severities 2, 3, 4 (45 conditions) and, for the k-sweeps, 10 random draws per k. Test sets are small (20–40 good images per category), so one condition's FPR moves in steps of 2.5–5 points: read means, not single cells.

D3. QR4 and QR3: how many photos does it take?

Registered claim (QR4). AdaBN-style alignment from k = 10 target normals recovers ≥ 50% of the AUROC gain obtained with k = 50. Registered claim (QR3). The conformal threshold from n ≥ 20 normals keeps FPR ≤ α + a small-sample band in ≥ 2 of 3 categories, while a naive source threshold does not at severity ≥ 2.

{{CHART:ncurve}}

Mean false-alarm rate of the threshold calibrated on k photos of the new lighting (3 categories × 45 conditions × 10 random draws). The dotted black line is the theoretical E[FPR] = (n+1−k)/(n+1) from Section C1; the three measured curves follow it closely. The sharp rise at k = 5 is predicted by theory (below 9 photos the guarantee is void).

{{TABLE:qr4_decision}}

Reading the table:

  • The source threshold fails badly under shift: FPR = {{V:fpr_src_all}} on average (about 12% on clean data, where the same threshold was calibrated; by severity: 31% at s2, 36% at s3, 52% at s4).
  • B3 repairs it with ≈ 10 photos: FPR drops to {{V:fpr_b3_k10}} at k = 10, in line with α. This holds with no alignment at all: the threshold does most of the false-alarm repair.
  • B2 adds detection power at the same false-alarm level. At k = 10, TPR rises from 96% (B3 only) to 99% (B2a) and 99.5% (B2b). AUROC: 0.994 → 0.996 (B2a), 0.994 (B2b at k = 10) and 0.998 (B2b at k ≥ 25).
  • B2a saturates immediately: its AUROC and TPR are the same at k = 5 and k = 50. The mean offset needs very few photos.
  • B2b needs more photos (as predicted in C2): its false-alarm rate is high at k = 5 (≈ 38%) and approaches α only from k ≈ 25. It is not neutral on clean data (FPR 20% vs 12%): the std matching adds estimation noise.
  • Deployable ≈ oracle: "B2a" and "oracle (paired twins)" are indistinguishable (AUROC 0.996 both; TPR 98.8–99.0%). The pilot's optimism turned out to be small.

Verdicts.
QR4 partially supported: the "k = 10 recovers ≥ 50% of the k = 50 gain" part is true for B2a and B2b (median gain ratio 1.0, at least 0.5 in 100% of the conditions with a measurable gain; saturated already at k = 5 for B2a). The absolute recovery of the AUROC drop is only modest for B2a (≈ 48%) and larger for B2b (≈ 84%) on the 7 of 45 conditions where the shift cost at least 0.01 AUROC: AUROC is simply rarely damaged. The discolouration negative control was not run.
QR3 supported, with one weak category: with k ≥ 10 the realised FPR of the conformal threshold averages 7–11% over all conditions. Per category (k = 25): hazelnut 2%, leather 7%, bottle 12% (k = 50: 18%). So two of three categories are at or below α and bottle is above it. A 99% band should contain 99% of single cells; 76–93% do. Plausible causes, not tested: bottle has only 20 good test images (one image = 5 points), and the calibration photos are held-out train images, which may be slightly easier than test images. The naive source threshold fails at every severity ≥ 2 (FPR 31%, 36%, 52%).
