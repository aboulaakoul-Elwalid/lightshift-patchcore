F2. What could make the conclusions wrong (threats to validity)

  • Synthetic shifts. Five simple photometric transforms are not a factory lamp. Real exposure shifts clip and move highlights (D5), where B2 did not help.
  • Three categories, one split seed. Per-category spread is large (bottle behaves differently from hazelnut). More categories and seeds would tighten every number; we report means and a few per-category breakdowns, not confidence intervals.
  • Small test sets. 20–40 good test images per category; one image = 2.5–5 points of FPR.
  • Calibration photos are held-out training photos, which may be slightly easier than test photos; this can bias the realised FPR (bottle) and is not exactly the exchangeability the proof needs.
  • Image level only. We did not evaluate B2/B3 on pixel-level metrics (AU-PRO, SegF1); the AD 2 thresholded masks reuse the image-level threshold as a heuristic.
  • Own implementation of PatchCore (no 3×3 aggregation, no 9-neighbour re-weighting). The gate used anomalib; the study compares variants inside our pipeline.
  • B2's defect-erasing risk is only partly tested (contamination check; discolouration control not run).
  • External review (GitHub issues #1–#4). A reviewer found the pilot B2b bug, an inverted gamma comment and several wrong statements in the old pilot report; all are corrected (C2). Two open observations: the drift statistic has a high clean baseline on texture (leather), so a fixed "3× baseline" alarm is weak there; and B3 alone costs recall under strong directional shading.
  • One α. All experiments use α = 0.1; the miss rate at other α is not charted.

F3. What we would do next (ordered)

  1. Run the registered discolouration negative control and a B2 test on a dataset with dozens of normals per real lighting condition.
  2. B5: class-agnostic crop + higher resolution, on AD 2 can and vial (targets the tiny-defect failure, not lighting).
  3. B4: a second model, to show whether the false-alarm collapse is PatchCore-specific.
  4. Add confidence intervals (bootstrap over test images and seeds) to QR3/QR4, and a drift-based "recalibrate now" monitor (QR1 supports it).
