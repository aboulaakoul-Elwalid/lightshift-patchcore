A1. The task, from zero

Visual inspection and the labelling problem

A factory camera photographs parts; a decision must be made for each: OK or defective. Supervised learning would need many labelled examples of every defect type, but defects are rare, varied, and often new (a scratch no one has seen). What a factory does have in abundance is photos of good parts. Anomaly detection turns this around: learn what "normal" looks like from good parts only, and flag whatever does not fit.

The datasets

MVTec AD

15 object and texture categories. Per category: a train set of only good images, a test set of good and defective images, and pixel masks for defects. We use bottle, hazelnut, leather. Lighting is controlled and constant: the benchmark every method nearly solves (image AUROC ≈ 1.0).

MVTec AD 2

8 harder categories (can, fabric, fruit_jelly, rice, sheet_metal, vial, wallplugs, walnuts). Defects can be tiny, poses vary, and the test sets contain real lighting changes (file names carry the tag: regular, overexposed, underexposed, shift_1…). Hidden "private" test sets are scored on MVTec's server.

The sujet (LightShift)

Asks: make PatchCore robust to lighting shift without retraining. Proposed tools: B1 augmentation, B2 statistics alignment, B3 conformal threshold, B4 a different model for comparison; evaluate on MVTec AD, validate on AD 2.

Why "lighting" specifically

A model that was calibrated in the lab will meet a different lamp, a sunny window, a dirty diffuser. If a system silently starts rejecting good parts (false alarms) or accepting bad ones (misses), the operator loses trust in it. The sujet's question is therefore practical: how few photos from the new lighting does it take to restore a trustworthy decision?

Two separate failures, and keeping them separate is the central idea. Perception failure: the scores no longer separate good from bad (AUROC falls). Decision failure: the scores still separate but the cut-off is in the wrong place (false-alarm rate explodes). B2 targets the first, B3 the second.
