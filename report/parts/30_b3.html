C1. B3: a conformal threshold, derived

Goal. Choose τ so that a new good part raises a false alarm with probability at most α (say 10%), without assuming anything about the shape of the score distribution. Ingredient: n good photos taken under the deployment lighting, scored by the model: S1, …, Sn.

Derivation (split conformal prediction)

Let Sn+1 be the score of a new good photo from the same lighting. Assume S1…n+1 are exchangeable (any ordering is equally likely; i.i.d. is a special case). Then the rank of Sn+1 among all n+1 values is uniform on {1, …, n+1} (if there are no ties).

Let S(k) be the k-th smallest of S1…n. Sn+1 ≤ S(k) ⇔ the rank of Sn+1 among n+1 values is ≤ k ⇒ P( Sn+1 ≤ S(k) ) = k / (n+1) We want a false alarm probability P( Sn+1 > τ ) ≤ α, so we need k/(n+1) ≥ 1 − α: k = ⌈ (n+1)(1 − α) ⌉ τ = S(k)

That is the whole proof: counting ranks. No Gaussian assumption, no model of the scores.

How good is it for finite n? The Beta law

The statement above averages over the random calibration set. For one fixed calibration set, the true probability that a normal image scores below τ is F(τ) where F is the score CDF; and F(S(k)) is the k-th smallest of n uniforms, which is Beta(k, n+1−k). So the realised false-alarm rate (FPR) is

FPR ~ Beta( n+1−k , k ) mean = (n+1−k) / (n+1) ≤ α

The "k-th largest" typo in the sujet, and your max-vs-miss objection

The sujet writes "the ⌈(n+1)(1−α)⌉-th largest score". For n = 50, α = 0.1 that is k = 46: the 46th largest is the 5th smallest, a threshold near the 10th percentile, so about 90% of good parts would be flagged. The correct reading is the k-th smallest (equivalently the (n+1−k)-th largest). We fixed this in code (src/lightshift/b3.py) and tests (tests/test_b3.py).

And the point you raised during the project is exactly right and worth stating as a trade-off: a higher threshold buys fewer false alarms at the price of more misses. The most conservative choice, the maximum calibration score, minimises false alarms but lets weak defects pass. The conformal rule is the principled middle: for n = 27, k = 26, which is the second largest score, one rank below the maximum. α is the dial: lowering it moves τ up and the miss rate with it. The guarantee covers only false alarms; it says nothing about misses, which we measure empirically (TPR) in Part D.

Worked example

n = 10 calibration scores: 0.31, 0.28, 0.35, 0.30, 0.33, 0.29, 0.32, 0.34, 0.27, 0.36. Sorted: 0.27 0.28 0.29 0.30 0.31 0.32 0.33 0.34 0.35 0.36. α = 0.1 → k = ⌈11 × 0.9⌉ = ⌈9.9⌉ = 10 → τ = 0.36 (the maximum). Expected FPR = 1/11 = 9.1% ≤ 10% ✓. With n = 19: k = ⌈20×0.9⌉ = 18, τ = 2nd largest, E[FPR] = 2/20 = 10%.

What the guarantee does not cover

  • Exchangeability: calibration photos and future photos must come from the same lighting. If the lamp changes after calibration, the guarantee is void. That is why recalibration after a lamp change is a deployment step.
  • Synthetic photos are not allowed in the calibration set. If you add augmented photos, the scores are no longer exchangeable with real future scores, and the "at most α" statement silently becomes false. (Augmentation is fine for B2's statistics, which carry no guarantee.)
  • It is marginal, not conditional: on average over calibration sets. For a particular small set, the realised FPR follows the Beta law above and can be well above α.
  • It controls only the false-alarm rate on normal parts. Misses are not controlled.
  • It is at image level. For AD 2 pixel-level SegF1 we reuse the threshold as a heuristic, without a guarantee.

Exercise C1.1

(a) n = 30, α = 0.1: find k and E[FPR]. (b) n = 7, α = 0.1: what happens? (c) n = 19, α = 0.05: find k and E[FPR]. (d) Why does making α smaller raise τ and what is the cost?

Solution
(a) k = ⌈31×0.9⌉ = ⌈27.9⌉ = 28; E[FPR] = (31−28)/31 = 3/31 = 0.097. (b) k = ⌈8×0.9⌉ = 8 > n = 7: no valid threshold; cap at the max, E[FPR] = 1/8 = 0.125 > α: no guarantee. (c) k = ⌈20×0.95⌉ = 19 = n → τ = max; E[FPR] = 1/20 = 0.05. (d) smaller α ⇒ larger k ⇒ τ is a higher order statistic; fewer false alarms, but defects with scores in between now fall below τ: more misses.
